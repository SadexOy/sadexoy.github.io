Frequently asked questions about the regulations

Does the roof need fall protection?

By law, a building higher than 9 metres must have anchor structures for safety ropes. Falling from a lower height is dangerous too, so Sadex recommends fall protection for lower buildings as well.

How are snow guards sized?

Sizing a snow guard must take into account the roofing material, the roof pitch, the length of the roof slope, the roof structures and the fixing spacing of the snow guard. The fixing spacing can be used to influence the snow load the guard withstands.

We recommend always installing the snow guard along the full length of the eaves, because the snow mass of the whole roof will load the guard at the eaves. Particular attention must be paid to the fixing and sizing of a snow guard installed above doors or on only part of the roof.

If necessary, the snow load must also be reduced in line with the RT guideline and the Sadex warranty and maintenance instructions. For example, when installing a three-metre snow guard above a door, at least 4 brackets must be installed. The end of the tubes or profile then comes 100 mm from the outermost bracket.

How should safety rope anchor points be positioned?

Safety rope anchor points must always be designed for the specific building. The design must take into account the height of the building, the roof pitch, the roofing material, areas with a risk of falling and the location of the items that need maintenance. As a rule, safety rope anchor points should be installed along the access route on the roof.

Are wall ladders needed?

As a rule, wall ladders are needed if there are items on the roof that need maintenance. The regulations require that the roof of a building higher than 9 metres must be accessible both from inside and from outside. It must also be possible to leave the roof in an emergency, for example in the event of a fire. On lower buildings, fixed external wall ladders are in practice always needed if there are items on the roof that need maintenance.

Under the regulations, a building must be equipped with appropriate, durable safety solutions and equipment suitable for its use. In addition, the items on the roof that need maintenance must be accessible and it must be possible to work on them without endangering the safety of workers and bystanders. The building control authority requires building ladders to be in place by the final inspection before the building is occupied.

Which roof safety products does the law require?

Roof safety must always be designed for the specific building, taking into account the height of the building, the roof pitch, the roofing material and the location of the items on the roof that need maintenance. Under the regulations, a building must be equipped with appropriate, durable safety solutions and equipment suitable for its use. In addition, the items on the roof that need maintenance must be accessible and it must be possible to work on them without endangering the safety of workers and bystanders.

When do balcony pipes need a fire stop?

A fire stop is used when a fire class is required for the balconies. However, the stainless steel (RST) pipes tested by Sadex do not need a separate fire stop. The Sadex stainless steel balcony drainage system meets the fire classification on its own, without a fire stop.

Balcony drains are always made of stainless steel, and balcony pipes can be made of stainless steel or aluminium. When aluminium pipes are used, a fire stop is needed if a fire class is required for the balcony.

When do apartment building balconies need escape hatches?

Buildings always require an evacuation plan. The evacuation plan assumes that rescue from balconies is primarily carried out with the fire brigade's aerial equipment. If it is not possible to bring the aerial equipment close enough to the balconies, the balconies require escape hatches. Sadex recommends escape hatches for all apartment buildings, because escaping this way is considerably faster than with the fire brigade's ladder equipment.

Designing storm drainage

Storm drainage causes recurring problems in construction. Correctly designed drainage provides enough capacity for the volumes of water and room for adjusting the water flow.

  • A storm drain pipe that is too small, a wrongly positioned gully and the wrong gully model are common problems in construction.
  • The size of the storm drain should always be designed and built for the specific building, and the design must take the external rainwater system into account.
  • The drainage should always be the same size as or larger than the diameter of the downpipe that discharges into it.
  • The Sadex design team is there to help you design storm drainage.
